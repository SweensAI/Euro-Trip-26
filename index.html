Book before you go

Madurodam — timed entry required
Only sold online with a time slot, not at the gate. Worth booking now, especially for Jacob's birthday (Sat 8 Aug) so you've got a guaranteed slot that day.
madurodam.nl/en/tickets →
Belfry of Bruges — book a time slot
Gets busy, especially in August, and the official site recommends booking ahead to guarantee entry.
museabrugge.be/en/visit/locations/belfort →
Choco-Story — book online
Includes a live chocolate-making demo; booking ahead means you can pick a demo time rather than just turning up.
choco-story-brugge.be/en/tickets →
Bruges canal boat — no pre-booking needed
Tickets are sold on the day only, directly at the landing stages (e.g. Rozenhoedkaai) — nothing to arrange in advance. Just budget a short queue in peak season.

Bruges — hotel garage, no booking possible
Golden Tulip Hotel de' Medici has private underground parking for ~35 cars, €25/day. First-come, first-served — no reservation possible, so email the hotel your arrival time in advance to improve your chances of a space. Street parking nearby as backup.

The Hague/Scheveningen — hotel/nearby parking
Novotel The Hague World Forum has on-site or adjacent parking, roughly €22–30/day. Reservation isn't usually needed — this is the most straightforward of the three stops.

Tips

Sunday is the pinch point
Pack the car the night before so Sunday morning is just breakfast-and-go — the drive and ferry leave no room for a scramble.
Don't over-fill Bruges Thursday
If energy's flagging by early afternoon, drop the Belfry climb without guilt — Bruges rewards wandering more than ticking off sights.
Leave one slot unplanned
Ideally somewhere with a playground or open space. Great as a buffer if a drive runs long or a nap is needed.
Carry cash for parking
Some smaller town centre meters in France and Belgium aren't card-only.
Download offline maps
For each region, in case of patchy signal on the drives.
Eat your big meal at lunch
Cheaper, less rushed with kids, and frees up evenings to wind down.
🎂 Jacob's Birthday — Saturday 8 Aug
Falls right in the middle of the Hague/Scheveningen leg, so there's room to make it a proper day without extra driving. A few ways to shape it:
Morning: presents at the hotel
Let him open a gift or two over breakfast before heading out — no rush, no travel day to compete with.
Daytime: Madurodam as the birthday outing
It's already on the itinerary for this leg — frame it as "Jacob's birthday trip to Madurodam" and it becomes the day's main event rather than just another stop. The mini-airport and boat rides are the highlights for his age.
Afternoon: beach time + a treat
Scheveningen beach and pier in the afternoon, with an ice cream or funfair ride on the pier as a birthday extra.
Evening: let him pick dinner
A relaxed seafront restaurant where he chooses the spot — low-key but makes the day feel special without needing a big reservation.
Small extras worth packing
A few candles and a lighter/matches for a hotel-room cake moment, a couple of wrapped cards, and maybe a small inflatable or beach toy he can unwrap and use that same afternoon.

Lille
Old enough to enjoy the zoo properly and remember the animals — the reptile house especially. Good energy for a full lap of the Citadelle park too.

The Belfry climb (366 steps) is a good one-on-one adventure with a parent — Noah's too young for it, so it can be "Jacob's big climb." He'll likely enjoy the canal boat and the chocolate-making demo at Choco-Story too.

For Noah (5)

Lille
Free entry and a short visit length make the zoo low-pressure for a 5-year-old's attention span. The Citadelle's playground and pedal boats are likely to be a bigger hit than the zoo itself.
Bruges
The canal boat tour is short, novel, and doesn't require much stamina — a good fit. Skip the Belfry climb with him; the Markt square below has horse-drawn carriages and street performers to watch instead. Choco-Story's tasting at the end is an easy win.
The Hague/Scheveningen
Madurodam is still great at 5 — plenty to look at even without following every detail. The beach is likely the highlight of this leg for him: sandcastles and paddling need no explanation.
